## 3. Absence Policy — Confirm the 2-Absence Limit

**The issue:** The LTU 20% rule applied to a 10-week, once-weekly course = exactly **2 absences** before failure. That is tight for a graduate online course, especially given that students have professional and personal obligations over a compressed summer.

**Options to consider:**

- **Keep 2 absences as the hard limit** (matches LTU policy exactly, consistent with Su 25 language)
- **Add language distinguishing excused from unexcused** — e.g., a documented emergency counts differently than a no-show
- **Add a warning sequence** — e.g., first absence = instructor notification; second absence = registrar notification + grade consequence, rather than waiting until the third as described in the Su 25 language (which was written for a 15-week format)
- **Clarify the recording policy** — since sessions are recorded and posted, does watching the recording count as attendance? Currently the syllabus says no (camera on required for credit), but worth confirming this is what you want to enforce

**Current language in draft:**
> "Students can miss a maximum of 20% of a course. For a course that meets once per week this equates to **2 absences** over the 10-week term."

## 4. Intake Survey — Create in Canvas

**What's needed:** Build the actual intake survey in Canvas before Week 1.

The syllabus references it in Week 1 async activities:
> *"Watch course orientation video (Canvas); Complete intake survey"*

**Suggested questions:**

1. What is your undergraduate degree and field of study?
2. Have you used any of the following software before? *(Cove.tools / Revit / Rhino/Grasshopper / None of these)*
3. On a scale of 1–5, how comfortable are you with basic math and calculations? *(1 = not at all, 5 = very comfortable)*
4. What is one thing you are most uncertain or nervous about going into this course?
5. Is there anything the instructor should know about your schedule or circumstances this summer?

**Purpose:** Review before Week 1 to calibrate pacing, identify students who may need extra software support, and know who is coming in with zero design background vs. some prior exposure.
