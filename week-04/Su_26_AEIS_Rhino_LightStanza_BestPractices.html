Clean your model.
Then test daylight and electric light.

Your model has too much stuff. Extra layers and copies make bugs. Fix it once, then run both light tests. Do the steps in order. That's it.

⚠ Read this first — the #1 mistake

LightStanza throws away your Rhino layers.

So layers alone do nothing. LightStanza reads materials and display colors instead. Give each thing (walls, floor, glass) its own material or color. That is how the app knows what is what.
1

Clean the model

Delete the junk. A clean box of surfaces = fewer bugs and faster runs.

  • ✓Delete copies. Type SelDup → it finds objects sitting on top of each other → press Delete. Double surfaces confuse the light.
  • ✓Keep only what blocks light: walls, floors, ceilings, roof, glass. Delete furniture detail, plants, tiny parts, and far-away site stuff.
  • ✓Close the room. No gaps between walls, floor, and ceiling. Light leaks through holes and ruins the result.
  • ✓Fix surface directions. Type Dir to see the arrows (normals). They should point into the room / to the outside face. Flip any that point the wrong way. This matters most for glass.
  • ✓Check your units. Type Units. Make sure it is Feet or Meters — whatever you really modeled in. Wrong units = wrong light.
  • ✓Clean the file. Type Purge to throw out unused layers, blocks, and materials.
✗ Don't50 layers, hidden copies, messy blocks, thick fancy furniture.
✓ DoSimple flat surfaces. One wall = one surface. Clean and closed.
2

Make the windows right

This is the most important part for daylight. Do it carefully.

  • ✓A window = one flat surface filling the opening in the wall. Just the glass. Do not model thick frames or glass with depth.
  • ✓Make LightStanza see it as glass. Pick one of these two ways:
Way A — name itGive the glass a material named glass, window, or glazing. The word just needs to be in the name.
Way B — color it blueSet the object's display color to pure blue: Red 0, Green 0, Blue any number. LightStanza reads that as glass.
💡Also: make sure the glass surface's arrow (Dir) faces outside. Backwards glass blocks the sun.
3

Add a light grid

The grid is the invisible table where light gets measured. No grid = no numbers.

  • ✓Copy the floor surface of the room you want to test.
  • ✓Move it up to desk height: 30 inches (760 mm) above the floor. That is where people work.
  • ✓Name its material with lsgrid. Example: a material named lsgrid Office 1 becomes a grid called “Office 1” in the app.
💡Make one grid surface per room you care about. Keep them flat and inside the room.
4

Save as .dae (Collada)

  • ✓File → Save As. Change “Save as type” to Collada (*.dae). LightStanza cannot read .3dm — it needs .dae.
  • ✓Give it a clear name. Done.
5

Upload & check

  • ✓Go to app.lightstanza.com. Drag your .dae file in (or New Design → Import File).
  • ✓Spin the 3D view. Do the walls look solid? Is the glass see-through? Are the arrows/normals okay? Fix in the viewer if needed.
  • ✓Assign materials to walls, floor, and ceiling (their reflectance). Then pick your path below. 👇
☀

Path A — Daylight test

How much sun gets in. For LEED / sDA / ASE.

A

Run daylight

  • ✓Set your location (city / weather file). The sun depends on where the building is.
  • ✓Confirm your grids show up (the lsgrid surfaces from Step 3).
  • ✓Pick the study: Daylight Factor for a quick check, or sDA + ASE for LEED.
  • ✓Run it. Read the colored map: blue = dark, red = too bright. Aim for even, comfy daylight.
💡Not enough light? Bigger windows or lighter wall colors. Too much glare (lots of red)? Add shading.
⚡

Path B — Electric light test

Your light fixtures at night. Same clean model.

B

Run electric light

  • ✓Add a fixture (luminaire). Make a new one in LightStanza or pick from the library.
  • ✓Give it an IES file. That file holds the real light shape. A red warning in the 3D view means a fixture has no IES yet — fix those.
  • ✓Place fixtures in the 2D plan view (easier than 3D). Hold Ctrl to move up/down, Shift to turn off snapping. Use the grid for even spacing.
  • ✓Set the height and LLF (light loss factor) for each fixture in the Instance Manager.
  • ✓Run the illuminance calc. Choose Lux or Footcandles. Compare to the target for that room type.
💡Same grid rule: you still need a floor/desk-height grid (Step 3) to get numbers.
✓

Quick recap

  • ✓Clean model — delete copies & junk, close the room, fix normals, check units.
  • ✓Materials & colors, not layers (layers get deleted on import).
  • ✓Windows = flat surface + glass name or blue display color.
  • ✓Grid = floor copy, 30″ up, lsgrid in the name.
  • ✓Save as .dae → upload → check the 3D view → run.
