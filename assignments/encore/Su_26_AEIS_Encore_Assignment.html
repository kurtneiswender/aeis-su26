§ 01

What This Is.

The Encore is worth 60 points — more than all three Act Reviews combined. That weight should make you nervous for about ten seconds, and then it should reassure you, because most of the Encore is work you have already done.

Act III closes Tuesday 07·21. The Encore is due Sunday 07·26. Five days. You are not being asked to produce a fourth act in five days — that would be a cruel joke and a bad assignment. You are being asked to do three things: assemble the three acts into one coherent document, revise the parts that reviews and your own analysis showed were wrong, and integrate — to finally say, on paper and with numbers, where your three systems collided and which one gave way.

That third thing is the actual assignment. Anyone can staple three packages together. The 60 points are for the building that emerges when you admit that the acoustic cloud you designed in Week 2 is hanging exactly where your Week 7 house downlights need to be — and then resolve it.

What is actually new

Everything else in this package is revision. Only these are new writing:

  1. The Cross-System Integration Sheet — three documented collisions between systems, each with a resolution and a cost. This is the heart of the Encore and the single biggest scoring opportunity.
  2. The Week 9 Systems Addendum — the two systems we covered in the last lecture: sound reinforcement and emergency/egress power. One page each. Modest technical depth expected.
  3. The Final Retrospective — a closing reflection at the end of your Decision Log.

If you find yourself modeling something new, stop. That is not what this week is for.

Two pages, one per system. (b) Emergency & egress power: your egress lighting layer identified as the emergency branch, on its own circuit, with the 10-second / 90-minute requirement named and the 1 fc average path illumination shown. Modest depth — one lecture's worth, not a consultant's set.

Complete Decision Log — minimum 20 entriesRevise

The full running record, Weeks 1–9, assembled in order. Do not rewrite history — a log that shows you were wrong in Week 2 and corrected it in Week 6 scores higher than a log that was right all along. If you are short of 20 entries, write the missing ones now and date them honestly as written-in-retrospect.

Final RetrospectiveNew

The last page of the log. Wins / Struggles / Goals across the whole term, plus one paragraph answering directly: what would you do differently if you started this hall over on Monday? Specificity scores. “I would manage my time better” scores nothing; “I would set the RT60 target before choosing the geometry, because my fan shape locked in a volume that fought my 1.6 s target from day one” scores everything.

§ 03

What Counts as a Collision.

A real collision has numbers on both sides and a loser. If nothing gave way, it was not a conflict — it was a coincidence, and it scores as one.

These are the fault lines this building actually has. You do not have to use these three; you have to find yours.

  • The ceiling plane. Your Act I reflector cloud, your Act III house downlights, and your Act II clerestory all want the same square feet of ceiling. The cloud reflects early energy to the rear seats; the downlights need an unobstructed aim; the clerestory needs the roof. Somebody moves. Who, and what did it cost in RT60, in fc, or in sDA?
  • The absorptive surface vs. the illuminated surface. The acoustic panels that got your RT60 down to target are dark, porous, and low-reflectance. Your lumen-method CU assumed a room with reflective surfaces. Did you run the CU with the real reflectances of your acoustic treatment? If not, your fixture count is wrong — say so, and correct it.
  • Daylight vs. LPD vs. glare. The window that earned your sDA is the window that produces ASE, and the shading that killed the ASE also killed the daylight harvesting that was going to earn your LPD controls credit. Three numbers, one opening. Show the chain.
  • The quiet room vs. the loud equipment. Your NC target for the house was set in Act I. The AV rack, the drivers, and the electrical room all make noise and heat. Where did you put them, and does the hall still hit its NC?

Format each collision the way you would explain it to a client who is about to pay for the resolution: what fought, what the numbers were, what you chose, what it cost.

§ 05

Extra Credit — The Walkthrough.

Optional · 5-Minute Walkthrough Video · + up to 5 pts

Record a five-minute screen-share walking me through your hall. Not a formal presentation — no slides required, no polish expected. Open your drawings and talk: this is the room, this is what I was trying to make it do, here is where it fought me, here is what I chose.

What earns the points:

  • You narrate a decision, not a drawing. “Here is my RCP” earns nothing. “Here is where the cloud and the downlights collided, and here is the number that made me move the cloud” earns everything.
  • You are honest about a failure. The single most valuable thing you can say on this video is “this part is wrong, and here is how I know.”
  • It is five minutes. Not twelve. Editing is a design skill.

Submit as a file upload alongside the PDF, or paste an unlisted YouTube / Google Drive link (set to “anyone with the link can view”) at the top of your text entry. Scored 0–5 and added to your Encore total; it cannot push you above 60. It cannot hurt you — a weak video simply earns fewer bonus points. There is no reason not to try it.

§ 06

Rubric — 60 Points.

Six criteria, ten points each, on the same five-level scale used all term: Exceeds (10) · Mastery (8) · Near (6) · Below (3) · No Evidence (0). Mastery is full graduate competency — it is the expected outcome, and six Masteries earns 48/60, a solid B. Exceeds is reserved for work that would survive a real design review.

Note the weighting: Criterion 4 (Integration) is worth exactly as much as any single act. That is deliberate. The three acts are the evidence; the integration is the argument.

Late Work

The standard course policy applies: a 48-hour grace period at no penalty, then –20% per week. But understand what –20% means on a 60-point assignment — it is 12 points, which is more than half an Act Review. Submit something on time. An incomplete Encore submitted Sunday outscores a perfect one submitted the following weekend.

§ 09

NAAB Alignment.

The Encore is the course's culminating evidence for both Student Performance Criteria. Unlike the individual Acts, where one criterion led and the other supported, here both are primary — the integration requirement is precisely the demonstration that the student can reason across environmental and service systems at once.

SC.4B
Environmental Systems

Principles of environmental systems design based on qualitative and quantitative reasoning; application to problems involving solar geometry, daylight, lighting systems, and acoustics; use of performance-assessment tools; analysis across geographic regions.

SC.4E
Building Service Systems

Principles and appropriate application of building service systems — electrical distribution, lighting, life safety, and communication/AV; performance analysis through quantitative reasoning; application to practical problems using advanced technologies.
