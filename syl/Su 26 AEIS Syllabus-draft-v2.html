§ 01

The Invisible Architecture.

Walls can be photographed. Sound and light cannot. Yet the experience of a room — whether a singer’s breath reaches the back row, whether a lobby feels like arrival or like waiting — is governed almost entirely by these invisible forces. They are present in every section drawing without ever appearing in one. This course treats them as the primary clients of the building.

For ten weeks, every student will design the same building: a 500-seat auditorium with a prefunction room. Each student receives the same starting kit in Week 1 — generic seating diagrams (shoebox, fan, vineyard, horseshoe), a stage orientation, a site, a climate, and a program. From that kit, you will shape the hall three times over — first through the ear, then through the eye, then through the wire — with a fourth week reserved for tightening, and a tenth for the curtain.

Acoustics comes first because the shape of the room is acoustically determined: once the section and plan are tuned for sound, the daylight and lighting strategies follow. Daylight comes second, compressed to two weeks — its deeper treatment lives in the HVAC and Water Systems course. Lighting and electrical close the loop: the design we hear and see is finally wired, dimmed, and powered. Week 9 is yours for integration; Week 10 is the encore.

§ 03

The Decision Log.

Before the schedule, a word on the artifact that runs underneath it. The Decision Log is the primary learning record of the course — the place where your thinking about the hall is captured as it develops. It is not a transcript of what you did; it is the reasoning behind why you did it, and what you traded away.

You will maintain the log as a shared Google Doc (template provided in the Week 1 Canvas module — make a copy and share it with the instructor on Day 1). Update it after every weekly work session. The instructor has ongoing view access; the log is a live document, not a batch submission. Roughly 2–4 short entries per week is typical. At each Act Review, paste your entries into the Canvas text submission as a timestamped snapshot. Quality of reasoning matters more than entry count.

ReasoningRT60 script showed Eyring result at 1 kHz was 1.9 s — above the 1.4–1.7 s target. Added a 14° ceiling splay to redirect late lateral energy into the rear seating; recalculated at 1.6 s. Geometry change is visible in updated Rhino model.
Trade-offLost 6 ft of fly height above the proscenium; will reduce theatrical lighting throw options — revisit in Act III.
What I’d Reconsider(Filled in at Act I Review.) Should the splay be steeper at the back wall to avoid focusing echoes? Re-run the RT60 script with revised geometry before Act II.

A template document is provided in the Week 1 Canvas module. The log is evaluated qualitatively — the criterion is quality of reasoning, not whether your first decision was correct. A student who identifies a flawed decision, explains why, and corrects it has demonstrated more learning than one who happened to be right by accident.

§ 06

Tools & Software.

Primary — Rhino

Rhino + Grasshopper is the connective tissue of the semester. The acoustic shape, the daylight mass, and the lighting plan can all live in one model. The Python RT60 script runs directly inside Rhino; Ladybug runs in Grasshopper; LightStanza ingests the Rhino model directly via its web app.

Required Weeks 2–9.

Daylight + Lighting — LightStanza

LightStanza handles both daylight and artificial-lighting analysis in a single environment. Two access paths: (a) upload your Rhino model to the LightStanza web app, or (b) use the Revit plug-in if you prefer the BIM workflow.

Account setup in Week 4. Notify instructor of any license issues immediately.

Optional — Revit

Revit is fully supported for students who prefer it. Luminaire placement, IES analysis, panel schedules, and the Week 8 lighting+power plan can all be produced in Revit. Use Rhino.Inside.Revit to move acoustic geometry across.

Academic license via Autodesk Education.

Supporting tools: Python RT60 script (acoustic, provided on Canvas), Ladybug (solar), Excel or Sheets (load summaries, PV calc). Software access should never block a deliverable — email the instructor immediately if you hit a license wall.

§ 10

Catalog & NAAB.

Catalog Description

The course reviews the theories of sound perception, analysis of architectural acoustics, room acoustics, sound isolation, sound absorption, electronic sound reinforcement, and the study of acoustic properties of materials and room shapes to provide speech privacy, enhance speech clarity, enrich music spaces and control noise. The course also addresses basic electrical theory, utility connection options, emergency backup, distribution, and safety considerations as they relate to building construction. Electrical lighting is studied as part of an assimilated section covering the fundamentals of illumination, integrating natural daylighting and electrical lighting design for energy conservation. Class projects include investigations into course subjects to further enhance understanding of real-work applications.

Prerequisites

Architecture Foundations Studio 2 — ARC 5024
Architecture Interiors Studio 1 — ARC 5113

NAAB Program Criteria

Environmental Systems (SC.4B) — principles of environmental system design; application to problems involving solar geometry, daylight, natural ventilation, heating and cooling, indoor air quality, lighting systems, and acoustics; and analysis across different geographic regions.

Building Service Systems (SC.4E) — principles of building service systems (mechanical, plumbing, fire protection, electrical, lighting); appropriate application; performance analysis through quantitative reasoning; application to problems using advanced technologies.

Course Objectives
  • Environmental Systems: demonstrate principles of environmental systems design, how design criteria vary by geographic region, and the tools used for performance assessment — including daylighting, lighting systems, and acoustics. (SC.4B)
  • Building Service Systems: understand the basic principles and appropriate application and performance of building service systems, including lighting and electrical. (SC.4E)
Additional Learning Outcomes
  • Ecological Knowledge — dynamics between built and natural environments; resilience.
  • Health, Safety & Welfare — impact of the built environment on human comfort and safety at multiple scales.
  • Building Integration — design decisions that integrate envelope, structural, environmental control, and life-safety systems with measurable performance outcomes.

§ 12

Before the Curtain.

An auditorium is one of the few building types whose quality is measurable at every register of the senses. Sound and light are invisible — they leave no line on a plan — yet they decide whether the room works. Over ten weeks we move through the hall in the order it should be designed: heard first, seen second, operated last.

Calculations are not the goal. The hall is. Every reverberation time, every footcandle, every wired circuit exists to answer a question about the room you are making. Bring the questions to the lab. The calculations will follow.
