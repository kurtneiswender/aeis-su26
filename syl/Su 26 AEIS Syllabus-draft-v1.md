### Catalog Description

The course reviews the theories of sound perception, analysis of architectural acoustics, room acoustics, sound isolation, sound absorption, electronic sound reinforcement, and the study of acoustic properties of materials and room shapes to provide speech privacy, enhance speech clarity, enrich music spaces and control noise. The course also addresses basic electrical theory, utility connection options, emergency backup, distribution, and safety considerations as they relate to building construction. Electrical lighting is studied as part of an assimilated section covering the fundamentals of illumination, integrating natural daylighting and electrical lighting design for energy conservation. Class projects include investigations into course subjects to further enhance understanding of real work applications.

### Course Content

This course is part of the building systems series and covers lighting, electricity, and acoustics systems integration in building design. The fundamental scientific principles governing light and sound in the built environment will be examined in the context of human physiological, psychological, and biological needs as well as sustainable design perspectives. It exposes students to technologies, materials, and strategies for control of light and sound in buildings, as well as the basic analyses needed to inform design decision-making and examine project performance.

The course continues the themes of resource efficiency and end-user comfort through the examination of emerging metrics for daylight sufficiency, visual, and acoustic comfort. **This semester the course is structured around a single integrating design project — the Community Learning Center — that students develop incrementally across all 10 weeks.** Lectures and lab sessions each week provide exactly what you need to take the next step on your building. The project is the curriculum. The readings are in service of the project.

### The Integrating Project: Community Learning Center

Each student designs the environmental systems for a **single-story Community Learning Center (~3,500 sf)** provided by the instructor in Week 1. The building contains four spaces, each with a different primary system challenge:

| Space | Approx. SF | Primary Challenge | Secondary Challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading Room | 800 | Daylighting + glare control | Acoustic privacy |
| Workshop / Flex Room | 1,000 | Acoustic performance | Flexible lighting |
| Maker / Fab Area | 700 | Task lighting + electrical load | Noise isolation |
| Admin Suite | 500 | Baseline reference | Electrical distribution hub |

The building geometry, site location, program narrative, and climate data are provided. Students make all systems design decisions. The full list of decisions you will make over 10 weeks is distributed in Week 1 so the shape of the project is visible from Day 1.

#### The Decision Log

Every student maintains a running **Decision Log** — a living document updated each week with 2–4 new entries. This is the primary learning artifact of the course. Each entry follows this format:

> **Decision:** What I decided  
> **System:** Daylighting / Lighting / Electrical / Acoustics  
> **Space:** Which room  
> **Reasoning:** Why — reference to calculations, standards, or design intent  
> **Trade-off acknowledged:** What I gave up, or what the constraint was  

The Decision Log is evaluated at **Week 5 (midterm)** and **Week 10 (final)**. The grading criterion is the quality of your reasoning — not whether your first decision was correct. A student who identifies a flawed decision, explains why it was wrong, and corrects it with documentation has demonstrated more learning than one who happened to be right the first time with no explanation.

#### Final Submission

Due one week after the final class session. Submitted to Canvas as a single organized PDF package plus a video file (or link):

1. **Decision Log** — minimum 20 entries covering all three systems
2. **Annotated Floor Plan Set** — one plan per system layer (daylighting, lighting/electrical, acoustics), showing design decisions and key dimensions/values
3. **System Summary Sheets** — one page per major system (three total): key decisions, supporting calculations, and one honest reflection on what you would do differently
4. **PV Sizing Calculation** — from Week 10 lab
5. **5-Minute Walkthrough Video** — screen-share narration of your design decisions for one chosen space; this is not a formal presentation, it is you explaining your thinking to the instructor

### A Note on This Course's Approach

Graduate students in this course come from a variety of undergraduate backgrounds — many of you are encountering building systems design for the first time. This course is designed with that in mind.

Every week, the lecture gives you what you need to take the next step on your building. The lab gives you the space to take that step, with your instructor and peers alongside you. The Decision Log records your thinking as it develops — not as a compliance exercise, but as a genuine design reasoning document that will demonstrate, by Week 10, how far your thinking has traveled.

A few things worth saying plainly:

- **Calculations are not the goal.** Every calculation in this course exists to answer a question about your building: *Is there enough light in the reading room? Is the wall quiet enough? Can the roof carry enough PV to offset the lighting load?* Never run a calculation without first asking the question it answers.
- **Wrong first attempts are learning, not failure.** The final project asks what you would do differently. Students who can answer that with specificity have learned something that students who "got it right" by accident have not.
- **The tools serve your decisions — not the other way around.** A beautiful Revit illuminance map for a poorly reasoned design is worth less than a hand-drawn sketch with a thoughtful Decision Log entry.

This is a design course. The systems knowledge is in service of the design judgment you are building.
