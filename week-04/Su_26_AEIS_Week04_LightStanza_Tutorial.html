§ 01

Getting Your Model In

LightStanza accepts models from Rhino (Collada .dae) and Revit (via plugin). The quality of your simulation depends almost entirely on how well the model is prepared before upload. A few minutes of cleanup now saves a lot of confusion later.

Before You Upload: Model Checklist

  • Units are set correctly. LightStanza reads the file's embedded unit system. In Rhino: DocumentProperties → Units. Feet or meters both work; the platform converts automatically.
  • Glazing is modeled as separate surfaces. Windows and skylights must be distinct surfaces — not just holes in a wall. Keep them as single planes (no thickness needed). LightStanza identifies glazing by material assignment.
  • No extraneous geometry. Site context, furniture, and decorative elements increase simulation time. Delete or hide anything that is not a room-bounding surface or aperture.
  • A ground plane is included. Model a flat surface at grade level extending at least as wide as the building footprint. LightStanza needs this for accurate exterior sky calculations.
  • Layers have meaningful names. LightStanza assigns materials per layer. Name layers after their surface type: Wall-Concrete, Ceiling-Acoustic, Glazing-Clear, Floor-Wood.
  • Illuminate where you need data. In Rhino, assign the material name LSGrid to any surface you want to use as an illuminance measurement plane. LightStanza will detect it automatically on import and create a calculation grid there.

Uploading from Rhino

  1. Export from Rhino as Collada (.dae)

    In Rhino: File → Export Selected. Choose Collada (.dae) format. In the export options, check Export layers to preserve your layer structure. Do not use OBJ — OBJ does not reliably carry layer information into LightStanza.

  2. Create a new project in LightStanza

    In LightStanza, click the + icon at the top of the screen → New Design. Give your project a name matching your hall: e.g., Smith_Auditorium_W04.

  3. Upload the model file

    Drag your .dae file directly into the LightStanza 3D viewer, or use the import icon in the top toolbar. LightStanza will process the model — this takes 30 seconds to 2 minutes depending on polygon count. The model appears in the 3D viewer when ready. If layers came through correctly, you will see them listed in the Layers panel (left sidebar).

  4. Set the project location and orientation

    Open the Site panel (globe icon, left sidebar). Type your building’s city or address. LightStanza automatically finds the nearest climate station and loads a full year of weather data. Also confirm the north arrow matches your site design — this determines which facade faces which sun angle. You can rotate the building here if needed.

  5. Check and adjust materials

    Open the Materials panel (paint bucket icon, left sidebar). Each layer from your Rhino model appears as a material entry. Set reflectance values for each surface type:

    • Painted concrete walls: 50% reflectance
    • Acoustic ceiling tile: 70–85% reflectance
    • Dark wood floor: 20% reflectance
    • Glazing: set Visible Transmittance (VT) — clear glass ≈ 0.60, lightly tinted ≈ 0.40–0.50

    LightStanza uses reflectance to calculate inter-reflections. Wrong values produce wrong results. You can return to this panel and re-run any simulation after adjusting materials.

  6. Create an Illuminance Grid (if not auto-detected)

    Open the Illuminance Grids panel (grid icon, left sidebar). If LightStanza detected your LSGrid-named surface from Rhino, it will appear here automatically. If not, click Add Grid and draw a horizontal grid at workplane height (2′–6″ / 0.75 m above the floor) covering the occupied floor area you want to analyze. Set grid spacing to 2′ for LEED calculations, 4′ for design exploration. The orange side of the grid faces the direction light is measured from — face it upward for a horizontal workplane.

  7. Verify the model in the 3D viewer

    Orbit the model. Confirm: (a) building is oriented correctly relative to north, (b) glazing surfaces appear as distinct elements, (c) no geometry is floating or missing, (d) illuminance grid is positioned at the right height. If anything looks wrong, go back to Rhino, fix the issue, and re-export.

Revit users: Use the LightStanza Revit plugin (lightstanza.com/plugins) — supports Revit 2021–2026, not Revit LT. The plugin syncs your model directly and handles illuminance grid placement. Skip the Collada export steps above. Revit material assignments map automatically to LightStanza materials; review the Materials panel after import to verify.

§ 02

Running a Daylighting Simulation

LightStanza calculates daylighting performance using climate-based simulations driven by real weather data for your location. The two primary metrics — sDA and ASE — are the industry standard for evaluating annual daylighting performance and are required for LEED v4 credit.

The goal of daylighting analysis is not a beautiful false-color map. It is an answer to a specific question: does this space receive enough daylight, often enough, without causing glare or overheating?

The Two Primary Metrics

Primary Metric
Spatial Daylight Autonomy (sDA)
sDA(300 lux, 50%) ≥ 55%
Percentage of floor area receiving at least 300 lux for at least 50% of occupied hours per year (8am–6pm). Accounts for blind operation when direct sun is present. LEED v4 requires sDA ≥ 55% for credit; ≥ 75% for additional credit.
Constraint Metric
Annual Sunlight Exposure (ASE)
ASE ≤ 10% required
Percentage of floor area receiving more than 1,000 lux of direct sunlight for more than 250 hours per year. High ASE means glare and overheating risk. Must be ≤ 10% to qualify for LEED credit regardless of sDA score.
Supporting Metrics
DA, cDA, UDI
Context-dependent
Daylight Autonomy (DA) is binary: meets threshold or not. Continuous DA (cDA) awards partial credit near the threshold. Useful Daylight Illuminance (UDI) measures hours within a comfortable range (typically 100–2000 lux).
Glare Metric
Daylight Glare Probability (DGP)
DGP < 0.35 = comfortable
Probability that a person at a specific viewpoint will experience disturbing glare. Requires a rendered image simulation at a specific time and view direction. Run this for any critical viewing position (e.g., stage sightlines, reading areas).

Step-by-Step: Annual Daylighting Simulation

  1. Create an Illuminance Grid

    Open the Calculation Grids panel (grid icon, left sidebar). Click Add Grid. Place a horizontal grid at workplane height (30 inches = 2.5 ft above the floor for LEED compliance). Set grid spacing to 2 feet for LEED calculations; 4 feet is acceptable for design exploration.

    Draw the grid boundary to cover the occupied floor area of the space you are analyzing. For an auditorium, this is the seating area and lobby, not the stage or backstage.

  2. Set occupied hours

    In the grid settings, confirm the occupancy schedule. The LEED default is 8am–6pm, Monday–Friday. For a performance hall that primarily operates evenings and weekends, you may want to run a secondary simulation with a custom schedule to understand actual user experience vs. code compliance.

  3. Run the Annual Grid simulation

    Open the Activity Panel (right side) → click New → Annual Grid Illuminance. Select your illuminance grid. Choose simulation quality: High is appropriate for design; Very High adds computation time with marginal benefit for early-stage work. Click Run.

    Simulation time: typically 2–8 minutes depending on model complexity and server load. You will receive a notification when it completes.

  4. Read the sDA / ASE results

    When the simulation completes, click the result card in the Activity Panel. The false-color floor plan shows sDA performance across your grid:

    • Green areas: sDA ≥ 75% — excellent daylight autonomy
    • Yellow areas: sDA 55–75% — meets LEED minimum
    • Red/orange areas: sDA < 55% — daylight-deficient

    The ASE score appears as a single percentage in the results panel. If ASE > 10%, identify which areas are receiving excessive direct sun and consider adding shading devices or adjusting glazing orientation.

  5. Run a Point-in-Time Rendering (optional)

    For a visual sense of what the space looks like at a specific moment: Activity Panel → New → Rendering. Set the date and time (try the equinox — March 21 — at 9am and 3pm for LEED Option 2 compliance). Choose a saved viewpoint or create one using the camera tool. Run at High quality. This produces a photorealistic image you can use in your report.

  6. Use the Comparison Tool to test a design change

    Click the comparison button in the Activity Panel (two overlapping squares icon). This opens a split-screen view. In the second panel, create a new design instance — try adding an overhang, changing VT, or adjusting window area. Run the same Annual Grid simulation on the new instance and compare sDA and ASE side by side.

Interpreting Results for an Auditorium

Concert and performance halls present an unusual daylighting challenge. The primary acoustic and visual requirement — even, controlled illumination during performances — conflicts with the typical daylighting goal of maximizing natural light.

For your auditorium, focus your daylighting analysis on the lobby, foyer, restrooms, and support spaces. These are the spaces where sDA performance matters for occupant comfort and LEED credit. The hall itself may intentionally have little or no glazing — and that is a valid, defensible design decision if documented in your Decision Log with acoustic reasoning.

  • A lobby sDA of 65%+ is achievable and desirable
  • The transition from daylit lobby to dark hall is a deliberate acoustic and experiential threshold — name it in your design narrative
  • If your hall has clerestory glazing or oculi, analyze those as a separate grid from the occupied seating area

Blind Operation & Shading

sDA automatically accounts for blind deployment when direct sunlight exceeds 2% of the analysis grid. You can model specific shading strategies using the Blinds Panel:

Static Shading (overhangs, fins)

  • Model directly in Rhino before export — overhangs, brise-soleils, light shelves become geometry in LightStanza
  • Re-run the simulation after adding shading geometry; compare sDA and ASE
  • Use the false-color map to verify that shading eliminates ASE hot spots without penalizing sDA too heavily

Dynamic Shading (blinds)

  • Open the Blinds Panel → select a window surface → assign a blind type
  • Set to Dynamic for occupant-controlled operation (standard for LEED)
  • Set to Always Closed or Always Open to bracket the performance envelope
  • LightStanza optimizes blind operation to minimize ASE while maximizing sDA

§ 03

Electric Lighting in LightStanza

LightStanza's electric lighting workflow uses real photometric data from luminaire manufacturers in the form of IES files — the same files used by professional lighting designers. The simulation calculates illuminance based on fixture position, aiming angle, and the reflectance of surrounding surfaces.

IES files are the nutritional label of a luminaire: they contain the photometric data a simulation needs to calculate accurate illuminance. Always download the actual IES file from the manufacturer, not a generic substitute.

Understanding IES Files

An IES (Illuminating Engineering Society) file describes how a luminaire distributes light in three-dimensional space — candela values in every direction. LightStanza uses this data to calculate the resulting illuminance on your calculation grid, accounting for distance, angle, and surface reflectance.

Where to Get IES Files

  • Manufacturer websites (most major brands: Lithonia, Cooper, Selux, Zumtobel, Acuity)
  • Search: [brand name] IES download
  • The IES file name usually includes the product code; match it to the spec sheet
  • Download the spec sheet alongside the IES file — you need both for your deliverable

Fixture Selection Criteria

  • CCT: 2700–3000K for warm/intimate spaces; 3500–4000K for task/work areas
  • CRI: ≥ 80 for general use; ≥ 90 for performance or critical visual tasks
  • Distribution: direct (downlight), indirect (uplight), or direct/indirect
  • Efficacy: lumens per watt — higher is more energy-efficient

Step-by-Step: Electric Lighting Simulation

  1. Open the IES Manager

    In LightStanza, open the Luminaires Panel (light bulb icon, left sidebar). Click IES Manager at the top. This is your library of photometric files for this project. Click Add IES File and upload the IES file you downloaded from the manufacturer.

    Once uploaded, the IES viewer shows the luminaire's distribution pattern (candela polar diagram) and total lumens. Verify it matches what you expect — a downlight should show a downward cone, a linear fixture a longitudinal distribution.

  2. Add fixtures from the Luminaire Manager

    In the Luminaires Panel, click Add Fixture. Select your IES file from the library. A new fixture instance appears on the model. You can set:

    • Height: mounting height above the floor (ceiling plane)
    • Aiming: tilt and rotation for adjustable fixtures
    • Color: CCT for visualization purposes (does not affect calculated illuminance)
    • LLF: Light Loss Factor (default 0.82 accounts for lamp aging and dirt)
  3. Place fixtures in the 3D viewer

    In the 3D viewer, click to place fixture instances on ceiling surfaces. Use the Instance Manager within the Luminaires Panel to copy, move, and organize fixtures. For a uniform layout: place one fixture, then use the copy/array tools to create a regular grid at your target spacing.

    Target spacing: For a 10 ft ceiling with downlights, start with fixtures spaced at 8–10 ft on center and adjust based on simulation results. For a 30 ft concert hall ceiling, spacing will be much wider; consider supplemental local lighting.

  4. Create a calculation grid for electric lighting

    You may use the same illuminance grid from your daylighting analysis, or create a new one at the appropriate workplane height for the space. For an auditorium seating area: workplane at 30 inches (seat height). For a lobby: 30 inches (standing/walking plane).

  5. Run the Electric Lighting simulation

    Activity Panel → New → Grid Illuminance (Electric Light Only). Select your luminaire group and your calculation grid. Set units to Footcandles (for imperial projects) or Lux. Click Run.

    Results appear as a false-color plan showing illuminance values across the grid. The panel also shows minimum, maximum, and average footcandles, plus uniformity ratio (min/avg).

  6. Check against target illuminance levels

    Compare your average and minimum footcandle values against standard targets:

    • Concert hall seating (ambient): 5–15 fc (low — performance mode)
    • Concert hall seating (intermission / housekeeping): 20–30 fc
    • Lobby / foyer (general): 20–30 fc
    • Box office / registration desk: 50 fc
    • Backstage / service: 30–50 fc
    • Stage (performance lighting, not modeled here): specialized design, outside this tutorial scope

    If results are too low, reduce fixture spacing or increase lumens. If too high or non-uniform, adjust layout or add dimming.

  7. Iterate: adjust layout and re-run

    LightStanza simulations run quickly enough to support real iteration. Change fixture spacing, try a different IES file, or adjust mounting height — re-run and compare results side by side using the Comparison Tool. Record each meaningful iteration as a Decision Log entry: what you changed, why, and what the result was.

Combined Daylighting + Electric Lighting

LightStanza can show daylighting and electric lighting results separately, but cannot currently run them simultaneously as a combined simulation. To understand how they complement each other:

  • Overlay your sDA false-color plan and your electric footcandle plan in the Comparison Tool
  • Areas with strong sDA (≥ 75%) can be served by daylight-dimming controls — your electric fixture load in those zones drops to near zero during occupied daytime hours
  • Areas with weak sDA (< 55%) need full electric lighting during all occupied hours — these zones drive your connected load calculation for the Week 6 electrical analysis
  • Document this zone map in your annotated floor plan: daylight fill zone vs. electric fill zone

§ 04

Generating & Sharing Reports

LightStanza generates both 2D PDF reports and shareable 3D web reports. For your Week 4 deliverable, you will submit both a 2D report (as PDF) and annotate a floor plan screenshot in your submission package.

  1. Generate a 2D Report

    Activity Panel → click the completed simulation card → click Report (document icon). The 2D report includes: false-color floor plan, metric summary (sDA%, ASE%, average fc), simulation settings, and project information. Download as PDF.

  2. Generate a 3D Report (shareable link)

    From the 3D viewer, click the Share button (top toolbar) → Share 3D Report. This creates a link that anyone can open in a browser to view your model with simulation results overlaid — useful for presentations and for submitting to Canvas.

  3. Annotate the floor plan

    Take a screenshot of the false-color sDA result and the false-color electric footcandle result. Open both in your drawing software. On a single plan, mark: (a) the daylight fill zone (sDA ≥ 55%), (b) the electric fill zone (sDA < 55%), (c) your fixture layout, and (d) key values (sDA%, ASE%, avg fc at each zone). This annotated plan is the primary visual deliverable.

  4. Download raw data (optional)

    Activity Panel → completed simulation card → Download. You receive a ZIP containing illuminance values at each grid point in CSV format. This data can be imported into Excel if you need to verify specific point values or produce custom charts.
