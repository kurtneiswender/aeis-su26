## Criterion 1 — Lighting Design & Illuminance
**NAAB SC.4B + SC.4E** · *4 points*

> Do the lumen-method calculations and the illuminance analysis show that each space hits its IES maintained target? Does a reflected ceiling / lighting plan present the hall as layered systems on separate controls?

| Rating | Pts | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Exceeds** | 4 | Lumen-method calculations are shown for the key spaces (house, prefunction, and stage/work light), each with a cited IES maintained target (fc + lux) and the achieved E reported. Results are verified against a LightStanza or Revit false-color illuminance map; any discrepancy between hand calc and simulation is explained. A reflected ceiling or lighting plan shows the four layers — house, egress, work, accent — each assigned to its own control zone, with fixture locations and a legend. The plan reads as a designed system, not a uniform grid. |
| **Mastery** | 3 | Lumen-method calcs are present for at least two spaces with targets cited and achieved E reported. An illuminance map is included. The lighting plan shows the layers and controls with minor gaps — one layer or control zone is unclear, or simulation and hand calc are not directly compared. |
| **Near** | 2 | A single calc or a generic illuminance map without comparison to a target. The plan shows fixtures but does not differentiate the layers or their controls. Lighting is treated as one level for the whole room. |
| **Below** | 1 | No lumen-method calculation, or no lighting plan. Fixtures are placed without a target illuminance or any analysis to support the layout. |
| **No Evidence** | 0 | Nothing submitted addressing lighting levels or layout. |

## Criterion 2 — Luminaire Selection & Photometric Logic
**NAAB SC.4B** · *4 points*

> Are the chosen luminaires real products backed by photometric data, and is each one matched to its task and layer? Is delivered (fixture) output used, not lamp lumens?

| Rating | Pts | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Exceeds** | 4 | A luminaire schedule lists real, specified fixtures backed by IES files. Each row gives delivered (fixture) lumens Φ, input watts, CCT, CRI, distribution type, and mounting. Every fixture is matched to its job: narrow/direct over aisles and apron; wide/diffuse warm light for the house and lobby; high-CRI (≥ 90) accent on the stage; asymmetric wash on architectural surfaces. Delivered lumens — not lamp lumens — are used in the calculations. The selection demonstrates that distribution, color, and efficacy were design choices. |
| **Mastery** | 3 | Schedule lists at least three real fixtures with most specifications present. Distribution, CCT, and CRI are mostly matched to task. Delivered lumens are used. Minor gaps — one fixture missing a spec, or one task/distribution mismatch unaddressed. |
| **Near** | 2 | Fixtures are named but the schedule is incomplete (no IES data, or CCT/CRI/distribution not stated). Matching of fixture to task is weak or asserted without reasoning. |
| **Below** | 1 | Generic fixtures with no specifications and no schedule. No photometric data of any kind. |
| **No Evidence** | 0 | No luminaire selection submitted. |

## Criterion 3 — Electrical Distribution & Load
**NAAB SC.4E** · *4 points*

> Is the connected lighting load calculated, grouped into logical circuits, and organized into a panel schedule?

| Rating | Pts | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Exceeds** | 4 | Connected lighting load is summed by space and by circuit (W and VA/A). Circuits are grouped by a logical principle — by layer, zone, or control scene — and the grouping is explained. A panel schedule lists each circuit with its load, breaker size, and phase, and reports the total connected load and a reasonable demand. Other building loads (receptacles, mechanical) are acknowledged with allowances even if not fully detailed. |
| **Mastery** | 3 | A load summary and a panel schedule are present with minor gaps — breaker sizing is approximate, one zone is missing, or demand factors are not applied. Circuit grouping is reasonable. |
| **Near** | 2 | A partial load list or a circuiting diagram, but no organized panel schedule. Loads are not totaled, or grouping logic is absent. |
| **Below** | 1 | Loads are mentioned without calculation, or no distribution work beyond naming a panel. |
| **No Evidence** | 0 | No electrical distribution work submitted. |

## Criterion 4 — Energy Performance: LPD & PV
**NAAB SC.4B + SC.4E** · *4 points*

> Does the installed lighting meet its energy-code power-density allowance, and is a PV array sized against a stated load offset?

| Rating | Pts | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Exceeds** | 4 | Installed Lighting Power Density (W/ft²) is calculated and compared explicitly to the ASHRAE 90.1 / IECC allowance for the space type, with a clear pass/fail. If over, a specific design response is proposed (higher-efficacy fixtures, fewer fixtures, controls credit). A PV array is sized to offset a stated fraction of the building's lighting (or total) load: the calculation shows the target offset, array size in kW, module count, and approximate roof area required, with assumptions (panel wattage, sun-hours) noted. |
| **Mastery** | 3 | Both an LPD comparison to an allowance and a PV sizing calculation are present, with minor gaps in derivation or stated assumptions. |
| **Near** | 2 | One of the two (LPD *or* PV) is addressed but not both, or a value is stated without a calculation behind it. |
| **Below** | 1 | Energy is mentioned without any numbers — no LPD figure, no PV size. |
| **No Evidence** | 0 | Neither LPD nor PV is addressed. |

## Criterion 5 — Decision Log Quality
**NAAB SC.4B** · *4 points*

> Do the Act III log entries show that lighting and electrical decisions were driven by analysis and by the systems already designed in Acts I and II?

| Rating | Pts | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Exceeds** | 4 | Four or more entries covering Weeks 6–8. At least one entry references a specific number or result (e.g., "house came in at 11 fc against a 12 fc target with 18 downlights; raised Φ to 4,000 lm and cut to 14 fixtures to hold target and trim installed watts"). At least one entry names a cross-system trade-off: a lighting layer that reveals or fights an Act I acoustic surface, an electric layer coordinated with the Act II daylight-harvesting strategy, or an LPD constraint that changed a fixture choice. Reasoning is in the student's own voice and shows that data drove the decision. |
| **Mastery** | 3 | Three or more entries covering Weeks 6–8. At least one connects a decision to a calculation or result. At least one cross-system trade-off is acknowledged. Trade-off field completed on most entries. |
| **Near** | 2 | Two entries, or entries describe what was done without referencing analysis results. No illuminance, LPD, or load numbers appear. No acknowledgment of the link to acoustics or daylight. |
| **Below** | 1 | Zero or one entry, or entries are placeholder text. No connection between the log and the lighting/electrical work in the PDF package. |
| **No Evidence** | 0 | No Decision Log entries submitted for Act III. |
